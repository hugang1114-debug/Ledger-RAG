# Research Claim

## Main Claim

Ledger-RAG should be evaluated as a deterministic evidence-ledger architecture for deep-research agents. The first-version research claim is that moving evidence into a versioned, replayable ledger with stable span identifiers, explicit claim-to-span mappings, and independent verification can improve evidence traceability, auditability, and long-task stability at acceptable engineering cost.

This claim is intentionally narrower than saying Ledger-RAG is a universal long-context replacement. It is also narrower than saying Ledger-RAG always improves final answer accuracy. A valid result may show stronger citation support and auditability with modest or mixed answer-quality changes.

## Negative Scope

The v1 paper must not claim:

- Ledger-RAG is a universal solution for long-context reasoning.
- Ledger-RAG fixes retrieval misses.
- Ledger-RAG guarantees answer accuracy in every domain.
- Token-level attribution is solved.
- Dynamic web browsing is reproducible enough for the main experiment.
- TRACE-style reinforcement learning is an executable v1 baseline.
- LOCA-bench is part of the first-batch main comparison.

TRACE and LOCA-bench should remain related-work or later-stage stress-test references until their reproduction cost, data requirements, and implementation burden are explicitly accepted.

## Falsifiability

The core claim is not supported if Ledger-RAG only improves answer quality while attribution and support metrics remain flat or worse. The claim is also not supported if gains disappear under oracle retrieval, cannot be replayed from recorded spans, or depend on unverifiable citation text generated by the model.

The claim is weakened if the full system improves support metrics only by refusing most answers, or if latency and cost grow enough to make the architecture impractical for research-agent workflows.

## Success Criteria

A successful first-version result should show:

- higher citation support or lower unsupported-claim rate than citation-only and hybrid RAG baselines
- replayable source/span evidence for generated claims
- stable behavior under longer contexts, evidence-position shifts, or noise stress tests
- clear separation between retrieval effects, ledger effects, and verifier effects
- reported latency, cost, and ledger/index size

## Failure Criteria

The paper should report a negative or mixed result if:

- attribution metrics do not improve over citation-only baselines
- support improvements come only from a stronger retriever
- source spans cannot be replayed from recorded hashes and paths
- verifier judgments are too unstable to support the claim
- system cost dominates the measured reliability benefit
